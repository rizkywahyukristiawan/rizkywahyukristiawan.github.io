01Positioning

Most ERP problems are not technical problems

They are translation problems. A finance team describes a payroll rule the way they live it; the system expects it as configuration. Someone has to sit in both conversations and carry the meaning across without losing it. That is the work I do, and I do it across the whole span rather than handing off at the boundary.

07About

About

I started on the technical side. Servers, operating systems, the parts of an ERP deployment that either work or do not. For five years at PT Intelegensia Mustaka Indonesia I installed and configured QAD ERP for manufacturing, pharmaceutical, chemical, and distribution clients across Indonesia and Singapore, kept the Linux estate underneath it running, and handled one full migration from on-premise servers to Alibaba Cloud.

What changed the shape of my work was sitting in the requirements conversations. Once you have spent enough time troubleshooting a system in production, you start hearing requirements differently — you can tell which ones will configure cleanly and which ones are going to become a workaround. That is what pulled me toward the functional side.

With Odoo I have held both roles on the same engagement: gathering requirements from the client, then building the custom module myself. An integration with QuickBooks for Emasjid in Singapore, aircraft booking and maintenance log modules for Vision Flight. No handover in the middle, which means nothing gets lost in it.

Now I work as a System Consultant at Andal Software on HR and payroll implementations — gathering payroll requirements, running gap analysis, building the configuration database, and taking clients through UAT. Different domain, same job: make sure what the business needs and what the system does end up being the same thing.
