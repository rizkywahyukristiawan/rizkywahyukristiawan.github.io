01Positioning

Most ERP problems are not technical problems

A client describes a business process the way they run it. The system expects it as configuration. Someone has to translate between the two without losing detail, and I do that across the full span — requirements through to implementation — rather than handing off halfway.

Context

NOK Corporation ran its ERP estate on its own servers across Batam and Singapore and needed to move to cloud infrastructure without interrupting daily operations, with no interruption to the business during the move.

My role

Technical Consultant — sizing, provisioning, migration, and cutover.

What I did

  • Sized and provisioned the target environment on Alibaba Cloud.
  • Moved the data and ran the cutover with minimal disruption to business operations.
  • Configured IPSec site-to-site tunnels connecting the client's on-premise network to the cloud environment.
  • Took over day-to-day cloud administration afterwards: provisioning, monitoring, patching, backup routines, and capacity maintenance.
  • Wrote Python and shell scripts to take over backups and health checks that had been running by hand.

System & technology

07About

About

I started on the technical side — servers, operating systems, and the infrastructure sitting under an ERP deployment. For three years at PT Intelegensia Mustaka Indonesia I installed and configured QAD ERP for manufacturing, pharmaceutical, chemical, food and beverage, and distribution clients across Indonesia and Singapore, maintained the Linux servers underneath, and handled the migration of NOK Corporation's Batam and Singapore environments to Alibaba Cloud.

Being in the requirements meetings changed how I work. After enough time troubleshooting systems in production, you hear requirements differently — you can tell which ones will configure cleanly and which will end up as a workaround. That moved me toward the functional side.

On Odoo projects I handle both roles: gathering requirements from the client, then building the module myself. A QuickBooks integration for Emasjid in Singapore, and booking and maintenance log modules for Vision Flight. There is no handover in between, so nothing gets lost in one.

The pattern across all of it is the same: work out what the business actually needs, then make the system do it — and stay close enough to the technical side to know which requirements will configure cleanly and which will not.
